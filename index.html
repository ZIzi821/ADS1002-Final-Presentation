Forecast design: how we tested models fairly

Use only information available at each forecast date, then keep July untouched until the end.

Four 28-day horizons (select one)

Jan 2013Jan 2014Jan 2015Aug 2015
history for profiles365-day model fit window28-day forecast

Leakage controls

  • Re-rank stores using past data at each origin
  • Build profiles from earlier Sales only
  • Fit transformations on training rows only
  • Freeze profiles for the full 28-day horizon

Information allowed at forecast time

  • Historical Sales profiles
  • Weekday, month and trend
  • Known Open, Promo and holiday schedules
  • Realised future Customers excluded

Models compared: historical profile, OLS, Ridge regression, decision trees and random forests. Settings chosen by RMSE, with MAE also checked.

Try it: which inputs are allowed at the August origin (31 July)?

Choose models on earlier windows. Use July only once as an independent test.

Thank you

Questions welcome. Open “Q&A extras” for prepared answers.

Why exclude Customers?
It correlates 0.755 with Sales on open days but is unknown at forecast time. Using it would overstate accuracy.
Why keep the high sales values?
They can be genuine promotion days. Removing them would make validation look easier.
Why keep the seven open-day zeros?
Their cause is unknown, so deleting them would be a guess. Missing dates are a different case and are never filled with zero.
Is the forest really worse?
July RMSE was 6.6% higher, but a 7-day-block bootstrap range (−320 to 81) spans zero. The evidence is inconclusive, not proof the profile is better.
Why do the open Sundays look so strong?
Only a small, selective group of stores opens on Sunday, so its 10,322 mean is not a typical Sunday.
Where do the planner’s daily values come from?
They are the fitted 120-tree forest’s daily totals from the no-promotion and every-weekday runs. The recent-schedule run uses exactly those values on promoted and unpromoted days, so the three report totals are reproduced. Custom mixes reuse the same per-day outputs.
